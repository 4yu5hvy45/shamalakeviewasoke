## 3. Recommended design direction for Shama

Shama is a serviced-apartment property, so the site should feel like a real hospitality brand rather than a generic AI landing page.

1. Use approved property photography first. Prioritise real room, pool, lobby, dining, and neighbourhood imagery. Do not present stock images as actual property photos.
2. Keep the hotel navigation familiar: Rooms & Suites, Facilities, Offers, Location, Gallery, Contact, and a clear Book Now action.
3. Make booking visible immediately. Use the official booking engine or its approved integration. A visual form alone is not a functional reservation flow.
4. Build a useful room comparison. Show real room names, sizes, bed configuration, occupancy, and the key differences between categories.
5. Treat offers as a real commercial module. Link directly to the official offer page and show terms or a concise qualifier next to the discount.
6. Use the brand palette with restraint. Let Shama's navy/blue identity lead; use red for a separate studio watermark/creator bar only if that is part of the pitch concept.
7. Avoid fake trust signals. Do not add invented client logos, review scores, awards, or booking-platform marks. Use only approved brand marks and verified details.
8. Design mobile booking deliberately. Keep a compact sticky Book Now/Offer bar, but make sure it does not cover page content or cookie controls.

## 4. Evaluation checklist

- [ ] Is the actual property obvious within the first screen?
- [ ] Are room and amenity photos real and approved?
- [ ] Can a guest reach official booking in one clear action?
- [ ] Are current promotions and conditions accurate?
- [ ] Does the mobile sticky CTA remain visible without covering content?
- [ ] Are room categories easy to compare?
- [ ] Are address, transport, contact, and policies easy to find?
- [ ] Are all links and form states tested on mobile and desktop?
